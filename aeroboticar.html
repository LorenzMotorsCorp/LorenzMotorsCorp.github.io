Aeroboticarbon

A two-seat electric VTOL with four shrouded ducted fans, a carbon frame and the body of a sports car. Built to fly point to point, and to fly natively on LAPP.

Concept render

The Vehicle

A car you'd want to own. That flies.

Most eVTOL concepts look like drones with a seat bolted in. The Aeroboticarbon starts from the other end: a low, two-seat body with the proportions of a sports car, and four shrouded ducted fans at its corners where the wheels would be on a car.

The ducts keep the blades enclosed, which matters when people stand next to the aircraft. A carbon fiber frame carries the load from each fan pod through the chassis, and a swan-neck rear wing adds lift in forward flight. Electric propulsion throughout.

It is also the reference aircraft for LAPP, the Lorenz Airspace Protocol. Its climb rates, energy envelope and communication stack are specified against the same protocol it will fly on, so the vehicle and the sky it flies in are designed together.

The 2027 Design

Oxblood, satin black, four fans.

The renders are where it's going. This is where it is.

The 1:5 scale Aeroboticarbon is a 16 lb coaxial octocopter with the production layout: four ducted fan pods, a carbon frame, a printed body and the rear wing. It flies a Cube Orange with a Jetson Orin Nano for autonomy and is entering tethered flight testing.
